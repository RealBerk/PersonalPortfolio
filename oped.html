European Diplomatic Edge and Credibility Can Only be Preserved if Yankees Go Home

Op-Ed Makale · 2026

The European decisionmakers choosing to follow Washington’s lead on the recent escalation of conflict in the Middle East is undermining her credibility as a neutral mediator and a values-based advocate for global integration. The intellectual blindspot of Europe cannot be addressed without acknowledging her own normative foreign policy objectives. Europe can only maintain her own proposed identity as a cradle of humanitarian philosophies and an advocate for a rules-based-order with disentagling from their commitments to the Atlantic Alliance. The recent escalations in the Middle East and the subsequent outpour of regional instability that distanced Gulf allies can only be halted with the EU`s recruitment of indigenous professionals who are familiar with the region concerned in matters pertaining to cooperation abroad. In contrast Europe is laible to misread any upcoming developments and add to the widening of the conflict in the region.

The recent developments that lead up to the Israeli escalation in the Occupied Palestinian territories and the United States’ unpopular and unilateral decision to break away from the JCPOA on the auspices of Israel are threatening and destablizing the global status quo into a ramping up of militaristic sentiments and polarizing the geopolitical sphere into increasingly unreconcilable camps likes of which the globe hasn’t seen since the dark days of WWII. We have a precarious frame in our hands in which global power players cannot afford to repeat the tone-deaf rhetoric pursued by several policymakers in the past. Europe cannot take for granted the USA’s and Israel’s goodfaith and assurance that their national interests are truly alligned with the EU. The objectives and the diplomatic patchwork being built by the American and Israeli statesmen of the past are being ravaged and auto-cannibalized by the current incumbent’s efforts to guarantee the survival of their own government’s political tenure at any cost. The era of imagining a peaceful coexistence that led to the Obama administration’s brokering of the JCPOA and the previous attempts of Israeli politicians to make a viable two-state solution possible with the Oslo Accords are setting in the horizon. The European governments were instrumental for both of the projects of thawing tensions in the past, either between USA-Iran or Israel-Palestine. If Europe doesn’t want to lose all the progress painstakingly won, she must come to grips with establishing her own stance separate from the Atlantic Alliance.

Right after October 7th the European administration chose to unequivocally stand behind the Israel government’s genocidal policies of extermination with invoking Israel’s “right to self-defence” (Politico, 2023a; Zachová, 2023; Le Monde, 2023) against small insurgent groups who in normal circumstances would have no viable chance at governance. This rhetoric that was pursued only exasperated the domestic legitimacy of both the extremist variants of political Islam and Zionism and hence only added to the moralistic allusions to a battle of great civilizations with opposing religious tenets. The real situation presents a more complicated picture where the current governments of America and Israel have had a essential hand in strengthening the case of more radical factions of either Iranian or Palestinian political movements. Their actions have made the reconciliatory factions pursuit a policy aiming at a cessation of conflict incramentally more untenable. Even the Europe`s own diplomatic staff was quicker than their administration to forsee the current policies would destabilize the region further with respect to Israeli aggression and neglect of Palestinian statehood (Politico, 2023b). Instead of a salient read on the crisis, the administration pushed the narrative of the hostilities commencing on October 7th even when the main message being relayed was attempting to curb Israeli actions that were clearly in violation of international law (The Guardian, 2023). Europe policymakers’ disavowal of Israeli actions would only come after the backlash of public opinion testifying to the brutality of Israeli policy (European Commission, 2026; European Parliament, 2026: European Union, 2026b). The European Commision only recently unveiled the Team Gaza Initiative on aiding in the relief on the humanitarian disaster that ensued (European Commision, 2026a) and the suspension of the EU-Israel Association Agreement and a call for military embargo would only come tragically late (European Parliament, 2026).

The current escalation in Iran started the US ceding from the JCPOA agreements and Iran commencing back their nuclear arms industry with the “Operation Epic Fury” as the American response under the leadership of President Trump and on the push of Israel on what has been controversially called a preemptive strike (Wicaksono, 2026). The initial strikes made any future effort on diplomatic peace even more diffucult to grasp with many of the Iranian government representatives being assasinated and the subsequent the Iranian military response on the US bases housed in numerous Gulf states and their economic centers (Human Rights Watch, 2026). The European Union was initially quick to disagree with the revoking of the JCPOA in 2018, regretting the Trump government`s decision to revoke the agreement that in their words was a “culmination of 12 years of diplomacy” that was ultimately “delivering on its main goal” (European Parliament, 2018). However after the active conflict ensued it has been an unpreventable disaster for European and American influence in the MENA region, the conflict was the spark that has enabled many Gulf allies to seek their security guarantees elsewhere, either the Saudi-Pakistan-Turkey “Sunni” axis forming with the Mecca Joint Defense Agreement (Turkiye Today, 2026; Dawn, 2026), increasing GCC countries’ cooperation within the region or the UAE decision to deepen strategic ties with Israel (foraus, 2026).

The diplomatic blunder and humanitarian crises that was been underlined can be explained through numerous ways however I find a decolonial reading and a close look at the elite structure determining the policy positions to be illustrative. Walter Mignolo (2009, as cited in Pitts, 2017) places decolonializing endeavours as concerned with “[for whom] and when, why and where knowledge is generated”, so he would prescribe “inverting the Cartesian presupposition of rationality”. This point shows the need to scrutinize the knowledge production that informs the high-level decision-making of core countries like EU member-states. It is important to consider that human structures, lives and histories constitute the scientific body of knowledge, and science itself cannot exist outside of colonial reality of biased human history in which science remains part of (Pitts, 2017). In light of a delonial reading I want you to consider the Ursula von der Leyen quote relating to October 7th: "It is an ancient evil, which reminds us of the darkest past and shocks all of us to the core," (Le Monde, 2013). The past being referenced is a European one however the future it is shaping is one of the Orient, such contradiction is how colonial policy is justified. Pitts (2017) frames Edward Said`s analysis of Orientalism as requiring “an implicit analysis of the configurations of the power between the West and non-Western geopolitical contexts”. Walsh (2010, as cited in Pitts, 2017) places the “white racialized intellectual paradigm as setting the boundaries “of who the intellectuals are and who are the intellectuals – and the intellectual authority of the academy and its ties to the state” so I find the problemetization of determining European foreign policy taking exclusive heed from its own intellectual past and production to be required in order to understand where the current blindspots lie. As Best, Lengyel & Verzichelli (2012) place the European unification vision as being predicated on the reorienting of European “policies of peaceful cooperation and economic and political integration” due to the prevelant and bloody intra-European conflicts of past, this fact has had an unforeseen side effect of a insular elite forming opinions to destablize the regions that lay outside Europe. The Eurelite seek to “develop a feeling of belongingness to a common European space and of the sameness with the elites in other European countries based on shared cultural traditions, belief systems” dictated and defined on elites’ terms (ibid.) only serve to continue the processes of Othering that has discounted the wider margin of the world prior. This cleavage of injustice and inadequate frame of reference can be struggled against with the inclusion of non-European partners to guide the European policy to aid in a swifter and more just response that would also truly embolden Europe’s international standing itself. This vision however cannot be achieved through the current organization and political vision of the Atlantic Alliance that has scorned Europe’s interests as illustrated.
